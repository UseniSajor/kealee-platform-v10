# Estates at Indian Head — desktop geotechnical screening and field program

Status: preliminary desktop planning aid, not a geotechnical report and not a substitute for borings, test pits, infiltration tests, laboratory testing, or a Maryland-licensed professional engineer’s certification.

## Desktop findings

- The archived county/NRCS layer maps HSG C soils across the site, including Croom-Marr and Beltsville/related map units. Map units are suitable for concept screening only; boundaries and properties must be verified in the field.
- The concept uses underdrained micro-bioretention and takes no infiltration credit. Do not remove underdrains or claim infiltration until field results support it.
- The base plan and county contours show material grade changes, particularly at Lot 1 and the Lots 2–4 front yards. Foundation bearing, fill suitability, settlement, groundwater, slope stability, and pavement subgrade remain unverified.
- No desktop source can rule out undocumented fill, perched/seasonal groundwater, restrictive layers, or locally occurring problematic strata.

## Minimum field program to scope with the geotechnical engineer

1. Perform at least one test pit or boring per acre of site disturbance for the proposed micro-bioretention program, with groundwater observations. Locate tests at representative M-6 cells; add tests where soil variability or proposed cuts/fills warrant them.
2. If any infiltration credit is proposed, perform infiltration testing at the proposed practice locations and elevations using the Maryland Stormwater Design Manual procedure. Keep the design underdrained unless accepted field rates, separation, and soil profile support infiltration.
3. Investigate each dwelling pad and significant cut/fill zone, with particular attention to Lot 1 and Lots 2–4. Provide bearing, settlement, footing, slab/basement drainage, lateral earth pressure, fill placement/compaction, and slope recommendations.
4. Investigate Estates Court, the Jennifer Drive entrance, utility trenches, and the proposed WSSC easement route for pavement subgrade, unsuitable soils, trench stability, groundwater, bedding, and reuse/disposal of excavated material.
5. Log soil/rock profile, samples, groundwater at drilling and after stabilization, evidence of fill, refusal, mottling, restrictive layers, and any Marlboro/Christiana clay, sulfidic material, or diatomaceous soil encountered. Provide laboratory classification, moisture-density, strength/bearing, and corrosivity testing as the engineer considers appropriate.
6. Survey every exploration and show it on the technical plan. Correlate results with proposed grades, foundations, SWM bottoms, utility inverts, and the final earthwork balance.

## Available resources before drilling

- Archived project sources: PGAtlas/NRCS soil polygons, M-NCPPC contours, the 2009 approved base plan, prior NRI/TCP limits, recorded plat and easement documents.
- USDA NRCS Web Soil Survey: authoritative current soil-survey mapping and engineering interpretations, while expressly requiring onsite investigation for engineering uses.
- Maryland Geological Survey / Maryland iMAP geology layers: regional screening only; known errors/omissions mean they cannot replace site exploration.
- WSSC 200-foot sheet 220SE01, utility as-builts, well records, prior nearby geotechnical reports (if owners authorize release), and contractor/subdivision records.
- Maryland Subtitle 32 / project reference code requires the soils investigation to correlate surface and subsurface conditions with grading/building work and address bearing, groundwater, and stability.
